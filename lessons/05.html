This lesson is a map of who makes the models and how their lineups are organized. The names and prices on these screens are a snapshot from November 2026. They will be out of date within months.

So the lesson spends most of its time on things that last: how to read a model name, how the tiers work, and how to pick a model when a new one ships.

The labs

Only a few labs can afford the frontier

A frontier lab trains its own large models from scratch at the edge of what's possible. That takes tens of thousands of GPUs, a large dataset and months of work after the main training run.

Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind lead. Chinese labs lead the models you can download.

A frontier lab trains its own large models from scratch, at the edge of what is possible. That takes tens of thousands of GPUs, a huge dataset, and months of work after the main training run. Lesson 02 covered the first two. Very few organizations can pay for that.

As of November 2026, three labs lead. They are Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind. xAI and Meta follow them. All five mostly keep their weights private.

The bottom group publishes weights that anyone can download. Since early 2025, Chinese labs have led that group: DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, Zhipu and MiniMax. Mistral, in France, is the main European name. Mistral's own model card says the Chinese open models score higher than its best one.

Ask which of these labs people in the room have used this month. Most rooms name two or three.

Models and products

The model is one layer of the product you use

A model is the trained network. A product is what you open: ChatGPT, the Claude apps, Gemini, Claude Code, Codex.

One model can power many products, and one product can switch models under you.

People use the product name and the model name as if they were the same thing. They are different. The model is the trained network. The product is what you open, such as ChatGPT, Claude Code or Codex.

A product wraps the model in a system prompt, a router that may choose a model for you, and a harness that runs tools.

So when someone says "ChatGPT got worse", any of the four could have changed. When OpenAI launched GPT-5 in August 2025, the router was down for much of launch day, and the new model looked weaker than it was. Lesson 08 covers harnesses.

Ask the room: your coding agent felt worse this week. Which of the four layers would you check first, and how?

Closed and open weights

Labs release models in two ways

With a closed-weight model you send text to the lab's servers and get text back. You never see the parameters.

With an open-weight model the lab publishes the parameters, so anyone can download and run it.

Labs release models in two ways. With closed weights, the model stays on the lab's servers. You send text and get text back, and you never see the parameters. Claude, the GPT-6 family, Gemini and Grok work this way.

With open weights, the lab publishes the parameters. Anyone can download the model and run it, on their own machine or through a hosting company. DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen, GLM and Mistral work this way.

The split isn't clean. OpenAI and Google each publish some smaller open models, and Meta has opened two of its Muse models. Open models mostly come from different labs than the top closed ones, and they arrive some months behind. Lesson 06 is all about this difference.

A top model for the hardest work, a workhorse for most coding, and a fast, cheap one for bulk jobs and sub-agents.

The chain

Open models trail the closed frontier by months

A capability that's frontier-only today tends to show up in open weights within months. Open labs get there partly through their own research and partly by training on frontier outputs, which is called distillation.

The measured gap depends on the task and the date.

Models sit at different places in a chain. The closed frontier labs are on the left. Months later, the open-weight leaders reach similar capability. From there the community makes fine-tunes and smaller versions.

Open labs catch up through their own research, and partly by training on the outputs of frontier models. That second route is called distillation. Labs use it on their own models all the time. It becomes a dispute when a competitor does it through someone else's API. In February 2026 Anthropic said it had found about 24,000 fraudulent accounts doing that, and attributed them to three Chinese labs. Those companies haven't confirmed it.

Epoch AI measured a gap of about a year across benchmarks in 2024. Its October 2026 study found six to seven months, for cyber tasks only. Neither number is a rule for every coding task. Compare current models on your own work.

Watch for "open models are six months behind" quoted as a law. Ask what task and what date the number came from.

The chain

Open models set a price floor

When an open model is good enough, anyone can host it, and hosting companies compete on price. That adds to the pressure on closed labs' prices.

The September 2026 releases started what Simon Willison called "a new price war". Haiku 5.5 followed in October.

Safety routing

The model that answers may not be the one you picked

With Fable, a classifier decides whether your request goes to Fable or to an older Opus model, and you're told when it does. When Fable 5 launched in June 2026, Anthropic said this happened in fewer than 5% of sessions on average.

For security tooling, dependency audits or bioinformatics, log which model answered.

When you pick Fable, Anthropic's top general model, you don't always get Fable. A classifier reads each request first. Most requests go straight to Fable. A request flagged as cyber, biology or chemistry, or as an attempt to copy the model, is handed to an older Opus model instead. With Fable 5.2 that is Opus 4.8 for cyber and Opus 5 for biology.

When Fable 5 launched in June 2026, Anthropic said this happened in fewer than 5 percent of sessions on average, and that users are told each time. It says Fable 5.2 blocks about 55 percent less often than Fable 5.1 did.

Most coding sessions never trigger it. It changes things for security tooling, dependency audits and bioinformatics. There, the same prompt can reach a different model on different days. If you compare runs or debug a change in quality, log which model answered.

Ask the room whether the lab should route a request to a different model without asking first. What disclosure would they want?

A launch chart tells you how a model scored on a set of tests. It tells you little about what the model is like to work with every day.

Claude Opus 5 is the example. It shipped on July 24, 2026 and led a major leaderboard, ahead of the higher-tier Fable 5. Early hands-on reviews were good.

Then people used it for weeks. Two months later, Anthropic's own announcement of Opus 5.5 said communication was one of the most common complaints about Opus 5. One tester found it used two and a half times as many tokens as Opus 5.5 on a large code audit. Opus 5.5 was pitched as clearer, more direct and cheaper.

The author's note is blunter: Opus 5 felt tuned for benchmarks and poor in daily use, and people liked 5.5 much more.

Ask what a benchmark measures that daily use doesn't, and the reverse.

Reception

Routers, bugs and governments can break a good model

A model change is also a product change. Routers fail, servers have bugs, export controls arrive overnight and announced models get cancelled.

If you can wait a week after a launch, do.

GPT-5 launched in August 2025 behind an automatic router. The router was down for much of the first day, so the model seemed weaker than it was. Around the same time, Claude users were sure the model had been quietly downgraded. Anthropic traced it to three infrastructure bugs and said it never reduces quality because of demand or load.

In June 2026, three days after Fable 5 launched, the US government applied export controls to it. Anthropic couldn't verify nationality in real time, so it suspended access for everyone for almost three weeks. And Google promised Gemini 3.5 Pro for June 2026, then never shipped it.

If your work depends on one model, have a second one you have already tested. When you can, wait a week after a launch before you switch.

Ask how much weight an announcement of a future model should carry when a team plans its tooling.

Re-test when a model launches or your current one starts to feel off.

What to remember
